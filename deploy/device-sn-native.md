# Device SN with independent Identity

`IDENTITY_DATA_SOURCE=identity` keeps account status, credentials, assignments and the policy graph in `IDENTITY_DB_*`. Device ownership and activation remain business data. Configure a separate `DEVICE_SN_DB_HOST`, `DEVICE_SN_DB_PORT` (default `3306`), `DEVICE_SN_DB_NAME`, `DEVICE_SN_DB_USER` and `DEVICE_SN_DB_PASSWORD` for the authoritative business writer. The database name has no implicit default. Give this connection only `SELECT` on that database's `device_sn` table. It does not need access to business `user`, `auth_assignment` or any credential table. Do not point it at an asynchronously replicated reader.

Missing device configuration disables SN issue/refresh and reports readiness false; ordinary account authentication remains available. The service never substitutes `LEGACY_DB_*` for the dedicated device connection. Device failures use `DEVICE_SN_*` errors so callers cannot treat them as permission to fall back to ordinary account issuance.

The existing internal request is unchanged: `POST /internal/auth/issue-user-token`, strong internal key, body `{legacyUserId, auth_method:"device_sn", device_sn_id}`. Device IDs must be positive safe integers. The device must belong to the same canonical numeric user ID, be activated and have a valid bound UUID. Issuance and refresh require an enabled device. Existing access may finish its original lifetime of at most three hours after disable; unbinding, invalid activation/UUID, account disable/deletion or elevation to `root`, `admin` or `manager` denies it immediately. Eligibility uses the current private role graph, including inherited privileged roles.

`GET /internal/auth/device-sn/readiness` remains internal-key protected. Existing `sessionSchema` and `legacySchema` boolean fields are retained for consumers; `deviceSource` identifies `device-sn-db` in native mode and `accountSource` identifies `identity`. Readiness probes are read-only and do not create device tables or grants. Deploy the business device migrations and private session provenance migration through the owning applications before enabling SN issuance.

SN credentials cannot mint OIDC credentials, change passwords/email, link providers or create/manage Identity accounts. Their provenance must survive persisted refresh rotation and every resulting JWT. Existing ordinary sessions without source fields retain their previous behavior. This change does not migrate or duplicate the business device table into the private account database.

The PHP SN management UI selects accounts through auth-key protected `GET /internal/auth/device-sn/accounts?q=&page=1&pageSize=20`. Its response is `{items:[{id,username,nickname}],total,page,page_size}`; only current private active, undeleted and unprivileged accounts appear. Search is limited to 200 characters, pages to 1–1,000,000 and page size to 1–100. Public plugin management authorization still belongs to the PHP live plugin configuration. Internal token verification includes verified `authContext` for an SN token so PHP can retain the credential restriction without trusting its business user projection.
